# From Backend Engineer

> **Level:** BEG → ADV · **Time to job-ready:** 12 weeks (10h/week)

## Your unfair advantages

- You already know APIs, databases, queues, observability, and deployment.
- You think in systems. Agent orchestration will feel familiar.
- You've debugged production incidents. Evals and SLOs are not new concepts.

## Your gaps

- You may have skipped the math of attention, embeddings, and tokenization.
- You may not have intuition for prompt sensitivity and context-window economics.
- You may underestimate how brittle LLM behavior is vs deterministic code.

## Recommended path through the rungs

| Week | Rung | Notes |
|------|------|-------|
| 1 | Rung 0 — Foundations | Skim transformers; focus on tokenization economics and KV-cache. |
| 1–2 | Rung 1 — Applied LLM Calling | Treat this like learning a new HTTP client. |
| 2–3 | Rung 2 — Context Engineering | This is your most important rung. |
| 3–5 | Rung 3 — Modern RAG | Vector DBs will feel like a new index type. |
| 5–6 | Rung 4 — Tools & MCP | MCP is essentially an RPC spec. |
| 6–7 | Rung 5 — Single-Agent Engineering | Skip the LangChain tutorial; go straight to OpenAI Agents SDK or Anthropic Claude Agent SDK. |
| 7–9 | Rung 6 — Multi-Agent Orchestration | Your systems thinking pays off here. |
| 9–10 | Rung 7 — Evals | Apply your testing discipline to LLMs. |
| 10–11 | Rung 8 — Production | This is your home turf; mostly new terms for old ideas. |
| 11–12 | Projects 1, 8, 15 | Pick projects that flex your infra muscles. |

## Projects to prioritize

1. **Project 8: Cost-optimized routing proxy** — your distributed-systems skills shine.
2. **Project 15: Incident-response autopilot** — you already know incident response.
3. **Project 5: Multi-agent research pipeline** — orchestration-heavy.

## Skip (for now)

- Rung 9 (Fine-tuning) — high cost, low ROI until you've shipped 2+ projects.
- Rung 11 (Real-Time & Voice) — niche; revisit if you target a voice-first role.
